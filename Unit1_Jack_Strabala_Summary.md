Summary:



This project involved an analysis of the NYC Citi Bike dataset using BigQuery and Gemini to uncover

significant patterns in trip duration, station popularity, and time-of-day usage. My analysis focused

on three key areas: understanding the difference in trip length between subscribers and casual

users, identifying the most frequently used starting stations, and examining how trip volumes

fluctuate throughout the day and week.



Key Findings:



Distinct User Behavior: A clear difference emerged in trip durations between user types.

Subscribers typically took much shorter rides (averaging around 800 seconds) compared to casual

users (averaging about 2100 seconds). This suggests subscribers primarily use Citi Bike for quick

commutes, while casual riders may utilize the service for longer or less frequent journeys.

High-Demand Stations: Certain stations consistently saw the highest number of starting

trips. Pershing Square North, E 17th \& Broadway, and W 21st \& 6th Ave were identified as the top

three, indicating areas with substantial demand that may require increased bike availability.

Commute-Driven Peaks: Usage patterns strongly correlated with typical weekday

commuting times. Peak activity was observed during both morning and evening rush hours,

highlighting that Citi Bike is a popular mode of transportation for work-related travel.

Actionable Recommendations for NYC DOT:



Optimize Bike Placement: Implement strategies to ensure a sufficient supply of bikes is

available at the busiest stations, particularly during peak weekday periods, to meet high demand.

Explore Tiered Pricing/Incentives: Consider introducing varied pricing structures or

incentives designed to encourage subscriber ridership during off-peak hours, which could help

balance system usage throughout the day.

Refinement Process:



During the analysis and validation phases, minor adjustments were necessary, such as handling

null values, ensuring data consistency, and refining visualizations to improve clarity. These steps

were crucial for producing reliable results and clear interpretations.



Personal Reflection:



Leveraging Gemini significantly streamlined the hypothesis generation and SQL query development

process. The combined power of Gemini and BigQuery allowed for effective exploration of the data,

leading to insights that could directly inform operational improvements for NYC DOT, ultimately

enhancing bike availability and the overall user experience.
