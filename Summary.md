\*\*Summary:\*\*

This project involved an analysis of the NYCCiti Bike dataset using
BigQuery and Gemini to uncover significant patterns in trip duration,
station popularity, and time-of-day usage. My analysis focused on three key
areas: understanding the difference in trip length between subscribers
and casual users, identifying the most frequently used starting stations,
and examining how trip volumes fluctuate throughout the day and week.

\*\*Key Findings:\*\*

1.\*\*Distinct User Behavior:\*\* A clear difference emerged in trip
durations between user types. Subscribers typically took much shorter
rides (averaging around 800 seconds) compared to casual users (averaging
about 2100 seconds). This suggests subscribers primarily use Citi Bike
for quick commutes, while casual riders may utilize the service for
longer or less frequent journeys.

2.\*\*High-Demand Stations:\*\* Certain stations consistently saw
the highest number of starting trips. Pershing Square North, E 17th \&
Broadway, and W 21st \& 6th Avenue were identified as the top three, indicating
areas with substantial demand that may require increased bike
availability.

3.\*\*Commute-Driven Peaks:\*\* Usage patterns strongly correlated with
typical weekday commuting times. Peak activity was observed during both
morning and evening rush hours, highlighting that Citi Bike is a popular
mode of transportation for work-related travel.

\*\*Actionable Recommendations for NYCDOT:\*\*

1.\*\*Optimize Bike Placement:\*\* Implement strategies to ensure
sufficient supply of bikes is available at the busiest stations,
particularly during peak weekday periods, to meet high demand.

2.\*\*Explore Tiered Pricing/Incentives:\*\* Consider introducing varied
pricing structures or incentives designed to encourage subscriber
ridership during off-peak hours, which could help balance
system usage throughout the day.

\*\*Refinement Process:\*\*

During the analysis and validation phases, minor adjustments were
necessary, such as handling null values, ensuring data consistency,
and refining visualizations to improve clarity. These steps were crucial
for producing reliable results and clear interpretations.
